Choosing a print-on-demand plugin for WooCommerce

Updated 18 August 2026

Plugin lists rank by feature count. That is the wrong axis for print-on-demand, where the cost of a quiet failure is a refunded order. Three questions settle it faster.

1. Which provider are you actually on?

Print-on-demand plugins are provider-specific. A Printful plugin does not talk to Printify, Gelato or anyone else — the APIs, product catalogues and variant models are different. Pick your provider first; the plugin follows.

If you have not committed to a provider yet, do that before choosing a plugin. Switching providers later means redoing the integration, not swapping a setting.

2. Which of the three jobs do you need?

  • Product sync — designs into your catalogue, prices and stock current.
  • Order routing — paid orders sent for fulfilment.
  • Live shipping rates — real delivery costs quoted at checkout.

You do not need all three on day one. Sync plus manual ordering is a legitimate way to start, and it removes the two highest-risk pieces while you have almost no orders.

3. What happens when it breaks?

This separates plugins more than anything on a feature table:

  • Does a failed run appear anywhere you will actually look?
  • Does a bad API response produce a half-written product, or a skip with a reason?
  • Can it modify products it did not create?
  • If it quotes shipping, what happens when the API is slow — does the cart wait, or fall back to your own rate? A checkout that hangs costs more than a wrong rate.

Reading the directory listing honestly

Before installing, open the plugin's WordPress.org page and check three fields: last updated, tested up to, and the rating distribution rather than the average. A pile of one-star reviews all describing the same fault tells you more than the mean.

For reference, on 18 August 2026 Printful's own WooCommerce plugin showed 50,000 active installs, 2.6 out of 5 from 96 reviews, last updated 14 January 2026, tested to WordPress 6.9.7. Check the current figures yourself — that is the point of the exercise.

Variants deserve a special look

If you sell one design in several sizes or colours, you need variable products. Some plugins create a separate simple product per variant, or import only the first one. Both look fine on a demo store with one design and fall apart on a real catalogue.

What ours does not do. The free plugin copies simple products using the first variant, so it is not the answer if you sell one design in six sizes. It does not send orders to Printful and does not quote live shipping rates. It works with Printful only — not Printify or other providers.
